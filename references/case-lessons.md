# Transferable case lessons

These lessons come from actual reference-coloring and print-part repairs. They are not universal dimensions, fixed part counts, reusable face indices, or evidence of physical print success.

## Appearance and scope

- Semantic colors preserve small iris colors and two-color labels; global clustering can erase them or turn shadows into extra materials.
- A raised nameplate includes its root and full sidewalls. A roughly cropped replacement can lose roundovers, add visible thickness, or leave a duplicate raised platform. Use the original surface and an inward receiver.
- Keeping a collar, shoulder ornament, or button's color does not require splitting it. Track the latest physical-part manifest independently from the palette.
- A projected selection can cut the body behind a backpack. Add visibility, depth, normals, and seed connectivity.
- If a reconstruction loses likeness, return to the accepted model rather than continuing to replace it. Optional retopology does not guarantee fidelity, an exact face count, or service success.

## Interfaces and support

- A nonplanar center-fan cap may be closed yet folded. Use shared boundaries and an appropriate surface construction, then inspect the underside.
- A nominally flat central area does not make the entire bottom flat. Check peripheral depressions, skirts, and folded lips, and use filling as well as cutting when necessary.
- A successful Boolean can leave only the locating pin. Check that the original main body and expected volume survive.
- Nearest-surface material volumes can create hidden fingers, enclosed islands, or undercuts. Geometric/directional interfaces may be more appropriate; validate the visible seam and insertion order.
- Static contact checks miss assembly cycles between adjacent accessories. Check the whole intended order, not just each insert versus its receiver.
- Small nostril cups can have perforated, unprintable rims despite a closed source shell. Inspect a close-up and local thickness; if reconstruction is necessary, preserve the outline and disclose the local surface change.

## Numerical and workflow lessons

Preserve negative internal cavity shells when they represent intended voids. Normal repair applied independently to every shell can fill cavities and create interference. Remove only diagnosed residues, not every small shell.

Avoid repeatedly cutting a very dense receiving body to fix tiny contacts. Prefer a supported local correction, a shared boundary, or a hidden-backside adjustment where appropriate. Repeated Booleans can accumulate zero-area triangles at file precision; switch method rather than loop indefinitely.

Reload final STL and actual vertex-color OBJ. Indexed internal geometry can pass while exported coincident points merge incorrectly. Record bounded simplification or local reconstruction and rerun the affected checks.

Keep the user's reported view fixed during a repair. Do not replace underside evidence with a attractive front render, or rebuild and re-export every unchanged part repeatedly. Maintain one accepted engineering version and synchronize final artifacts, images, and instructions at delivery.

Connectivity, closedness, selected intersection checks, pin-depth rays, and support sampling are useful evidence. They do not establish physical fit, exact likeness percentages, purge savings, or exhaustive adjacent-triangle self-intersection proof.
